Projects
Aug 2026 - Ongoing

An interactive, audio-driven digital patient that converses with trainee physicians in clinical interview practice, conveying patient-specific emotion and symptoms through photorealistic facial expression and gaze.

A psychiatry trainee talks with the avatar patient, whose replies come with behavior cues.

Psychiatric trainees need realistic virtual patients to practice clinical interviews. To feel like talking with a real patient, the avatar is photorealistic and responds in real time. Given a patient's response from a dialogue system, the avatar speaks it as a photorealistic 3D talking face, while remaining controllable per patient, e.g., reduced eye contact or blunted affect as symptoms of a condition. It will be used for psychiatric interview training.

  • Speech: Gemini TTS, prompted with a per-patient voice and the emotion label.
  • Expression: LAM_Audio2Expression predicts 52 ARKit blendshapes at 30 fps, used as FACS-style action units (AUs).
  • Patient & Emotion Control: Per-patient AU edits based on written clinical case studies, grounded in the FACS-based terms clinicians use to describe affect, with per-response emotion edits layered on top.
  • Rendering: AUs are linearly mapped to FLAME parameters driving GAGAvatar, a generalizable 3D Gaussian head avatar, fine-tuned per identity on 16-camera NeRSemble captures to recover identity detail beyond its one-shot reconstruction.

The system includes 25 avatars covering 87 patient case profiles from the training curriculum (e.g., major depressive disorder, first-episode schizophrenia, delirium). Below, one avatar speaks in six emotional states in turn, with each emotion applied to the AUs on top of the speech-driven motion.

In collaboration with Prof. Karan Singh and Prof. Venkat Bhat's team at University of Toronto.
